<Section icon={Trophy} title="Best Class Tier Rankings Overview">

Choosing the right starter class in Ragnarok Go shapes your entire early game. The six classic starting classes, Archer, Mage, Thief, Swordsman, Acolyte, and Merchant, each bring a distinct leveling speed, survivability profile, and party role. Whether you want to melt monsters from a distance or tank hits on the frontline, this ranking breaks down which class gives you the smoothest start.

The rankings below weigh three factors: **leveling speed**, **survivability without expensive gear**, and **party demand**. Classes that kill quickly, survive cheaply, and remain useful in groups score highest.

| Rank | Class | Leveling Speed | Survivability | Party Demand | Best For |
|------|-------|----------------|---------------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | **Archer** | ★★★★★ | ★★★★ | High | Fast, safe solo leveling |
| 2 | **Mage** | ★★★★★ | ★★ | Very High | Glass-cannon nukers |
| 3 | **Thief** | ★★★★ | ★★★★★ | Medium | Potion-efficient solo play |
| 4 | **Swordsman** | ★★★ | ★★★★ | Medium | Straightforward tanky start |
| 5 | **Acolyte** | ★★ | ★★★ | Very High | Support-oriented team players |
| 6 | **Merchant** | ★★ | ★★★ | Low | Long-term economy utility |

<Callout type="tip" title="Tier List Caveat">
These rankings focus on the **starting experience**. Every class becomes valuable later, and Merchant in particular turns into an economic powerhouse once funded by another character.
</Callout>

Archer earns the top spot because it demands the least from your resources. Ranged attacks mean fewer hits taken, which means fewer potions consumed, which means more zeny saved. Pair the right arrow element with the right monster and your damage output rivals the Mage without the fragility.

Mage trails closely behind. The learning curve involves memorizing elemental weaknesses and practicing cast mechanics, but the payoff is the fastest kill speed in the game against the right targets. If you enjoy a large variety of active skills and don't mind playing a glass cannon, Mage is your class.

<Callout type="warning" title="Mage Early Game Warning">
Mage suffers through the Novice stage before acquiring its first spell. Push through, the payoff once you can cast is well worth the slow start.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Mid-Tier Picks: Thief and Swordsman">

**Thief** sneaks into third place with an unrivaled ability to dodge. Once Improved Dodge ramps up, almost nothing touches them, letting Thieves farm monsters slightly above their intended difficulty. Double Attack provides steady, rhythmic damage output.

The Thief's strengths are almost entirely **passive**, which cuts both ways:

| Thief Strength | Thief Weakness |
|----------------|----------------|
| Unmatched dodge keeps HP high | Few active skills to spend SP on |
| Double Attack delivers reliable damage | Steal and Hide rarely speed up kills |
| Potions last longer than any other class | Less engaging for skill-spam players |
| Easy for healers to support | Slower kill ceiling than Archer or Mage |

**Swordsman** holds fourth as the quintessential warrior-tank. Sword masteries provide a solid damage boost, Improved HP Recovery cuts potion costs, and Bash, Magnum Break, and Provoke give multiple ways to convert the SP bar into damage. It is a straightforward, satisfying experience that groups are always happy to include.

<Callout type="success" title="When to Pick Swordsman">
If you want a no-nonsense melee start with strong group acceptance and forgiving gameplay, Swordsman is the easiest melee recommendation for a brand-new account.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Bottom Picks: Acolyte and Merchant">

**Acolyte** sits fifth. Self-healing and buffs improve survivability and slightly boost damage, and the class is the definition of group play, pair with a fast-leveling partner and you level just as quickly. In solo play, however, whether smiting undead with heals or fighting conventionally, progression is slow going.

**Merchant** ranks sixth for starting out. The earliest combat skill, Mammonite, costs **1,000 zeny per cast**, making it unsustainable early on. That leaves Merchants with a full SP bar and no practical way to use it, contributing little to groups, so expect mostly solo grinding.

| Class | Early Combat | SP Usage | Group Value | Long-Term Value |
|-------|--------------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Acolyte | Moderate | Buffs and heals | Very high support | Essential endgame support |
| Merchant | Weak | Mammonite too costly | Low early | **Economy and vending utility** |

<Callout type="info" title="The Merchant Exception">
Every serious player eventually needs a Merchant for vending and trading. The smart route is to fund one with a stronger first character rather than starting with it.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Map} title="How to Choose Your Class: Step-by-Step">

Follow this process to lock in the class that matches your goals:

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Identify Your Playstyle">
    Decide whether you prefer ranged safety (Archer), high-risk nuking (Mage), melee brawling (Swordsman), or support roles (Acolyte). Passive players suit Thief; economy players suit a later Merchant.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Assess Your Budget">
    Low-budget players should favor Archer or Thief, which consume the fewest potions. Mage requires elemental knowledge instead of gear. Swordsman's HP recovery also keeps costs manageable.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Decide Solo vs. Party">
    Solo-focused players thrive with Archer or Thief. Mage and Acolyte shine in parties, where their damage and support respectively multiply group efficiency.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Commit and Level Through Novice">
    Pick your class and push through the early levels, especially if you chose Mage. The weak Novice phase is temporary and pays off at the first skill unlock.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Plan Your Alt Characters">
    Once your main funds itself, roll a Merchant alt for vending and an Acolyte alt for warp and buff services.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Elemental Knowledge Pays">
Both top classes reward elemental matchups. Learn which arrows counter which monsters as an Archer, and which spell element melts each target as a Mage, and your leveling speed multiplies.
</Callout>

</Section>
